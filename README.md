# Wealthfront — FinTech Case Study
## How did a weak first hypothesis become a scalable financial platform?
An independent academic case study prepared for Introduction to FinTech at Università della Svizzera italiana (USI). It explores how Wealthfront evolved from its original kaChing concept into an automated investing business and, subsequently, a broader financial platform.
## Case study overview
The analysis follows three connected questions:
1. Strategic pivot: Why did Wealthfront move from identifying potentially outperforming investors to offering automated, diversified investing?
2. Finance meets technology: How did software help deliver established investment principles—such as diversification, rebalancing, and tax-aware investing—at scale?
3. Business model evolution: Why did Wealthfront expand beyond robo-advisory into cash management and other financial products?
## Key takeaways
- Focus on the problem, not the first solution. The move from kaChing to automated advice illustrates the importance of reassessing initial assumptions.
- Product-market fit is not the same as sustainable economics. Attracting users and developing a resilient revenue model are distinct challenges.
- FinTech innovation can come from better delivery. Technology can make established financial principles more accessible, scalable, and convenient without inventing a new financial theory.

## Scope and limitations
This is a qualitative, independent case study based on publicly available materials. It uses Malkiel's and Markowitz's ideas as an analytical lens; it does not claim that those ideas caused Wealthfront's strategic decisions. The case illustrates possible mechanisms and lessons rather than establishing causality.
Financial figures refer to the reporting periods identified in the presentation and should not be interpreted as live values.
